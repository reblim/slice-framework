# SLICE Framework v0.2

Revised specification · 16 October 2026

> **Archived version.** Superseded by v0.3 on 16 October 2026. Reproduced unchanged from the doc's revision 18; only this note was added.

## 0. Verdict and what changed

**Agree with the direction; ten changes before it is pilot-ready.** v0.1 is a sound delivery and forecasting framework. It is not yet a framework for the bottleneck it names: it treats planning as a cost to cut, while the thesis says deciding what to build is the scarce input. v0.2 fixes that tension and adds what 2025–2026 research shows about where AI gains actually disappear.

| # | Change | Why | Section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Split "planning" into coordination, decision and proof | The thesis and the Planning Budget pull in opposite directions | 1 |
| 2 | Planning Budget becomes the **Decision Budget**, with a new Build-to-decide mode | Cheap building is now the cheapest discovery tool; v0.1 only allows spikes | 6 |
| 3 | Every bet carries an outcome hypothesis and a kill criterion | v0.1 proves delivery, but nothing forces the team to learn whether the outcome moved | 5 |
| 4 | The spec becomes durable agent context, versioned with the code | Spec-driven tooling made this the norm; SLICE must say what goes in it and how much | 7 |
| 5 | Verification capacity becomes the hard work-in-progress limit | Telemetry shows review queues absorbing the coding gains | 9 |
| 6 | Forecast levels get minimum sample sizes; codebase type joins the reference class | Gains vary several-fold between greenfield and complex brownfield work | 8 |
| 7 | New metrics: decision latency, idea-to-evidence time, review wait, unreviewed merges, validated-outcome rate | Measure the bottleneck the framework claims exists | 11 |
| 8 | Explicit positioning against AWS AI-DLC, BMAD, Spec Kit and Shape Up | Proportional, AI-native process is no longer unique | 3 |
| 9 | C in SLICE now means **Capacity-bound** | "Confidence-led" and "Evidence-driven" overlapped | 4 |
| 10 | Normative voice throughout | v0.1 reads as a reply to a person ("I agree with your observation") and carries interface leftovers ("Copy feature card") | all |

**Kept from v0.1 unchanged:** the five-level hierarchy, Now/Next/Later, the four item types, XS–XL sizing without hour equivalents, AI leverage as a forecast input rather than a discount, F0–F3 evidence labels, Direction/Target/Commitment, the conformance-rule approach and vendor neutrality.

**On the research document:** it is a good survey, with two corrections. METR's February 2026 update did not report a speedup; it reported that its new data was too biased to estimate one. And its illustrative day ranges and multiplier explorer should stay out of the spec; v0.1 was right to drop them.

## 1. The thesis, restated

**Building is cheap. Deciding and proving are not.** The founding claim, that knowing what to build and when is now the bottleneck, is right. But v0.1 answers it with "plan less", which only works once "planning" is split into three different kinds of work.

| Kind of work | What it is | What AI did to it | SLICE stance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coordination | Estimating, ticket-writing, sequencing, status reporting, specs written for other humans | Cheaper, often unnecessary in small teams | Minimise. Budget it. |
| Decision | Choosing the user, the problem, the bet, the scope cut and the evidence that counts as done | Unchanged or harder: more options are now cheap to build, so more must be rejected | Invest deliberately. This is the new constraint. |
| Proof | Review, verification, integration, release, and learning whether the shipped thing worked | Harder: more code arrives faster than people can check it | Size all work to this capacity. |

v0.1 handles coordination well (the Planning Budget) and proof partly (section 5.6). It barely handles decision work, which is the part the thesis is actually about. v0.2 makes all three explicit.

Two consequences follow. First, when building is cheap, **building becomes a planning tool**: a throwaway prototype is often a cheaper way to resolve a decision than a document or a meeting. Second, the specification stops being paperwork for humans and becomes **context for agents**, so the right spec is worth more than before, while the wrong kind of planning is worth less.

## 2. Evidence base

**The evidence supports "building is cheaper" only unevenly, strongly supports "proof is a bottleneck", and supports "deciding is the bottleneck" mostly through practitioner testimony.** SLICE should say so plainly, and treat the third claim as the hypothesis its pilots must test.

**The gap SLICE can fill:** nobody has measured decision latency or idea-to-evidence time in AI-native teams. If SLICE pilots record them, the framework produces the first evidence for its own thesis. That is a stronger launch asset than the framework text.

## 3. Landscape and positioning

**SLICE should be the decision and forecasting layer above spec-driven execution tools, not another execution method.** Since mid-2025 several AI-native methods have shipped. All of them answer "how do we get agents to build this well". None answers "which slice, why, how much, by when, and did it work".

| Method | What it is | Overlap with SLICE | Where SLICE differs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [AWS AI-DLC](https://jellyfish.co/?p=35231) (Jul 2025) | AI leads execution, humans hold decision rights. Inception, Construction and Operations phases; work in "Bolts" of hours or days; Mob Elaboration where the team validates AI-written requirements live | AI-native lifecycle, short cycles, human accountability | AI-DLC adds ceremonies (mob sessions) and has no forecasting, commitment model or outcome kill criteria |
| [BMAD Method](https://www.nitorinfotech.com/blog/what-is-the-bmad-method-a-complete-guide-to-ai-driven-software-development-in-2026/) (open source, MIT) | Agent personas produce PRD, architecture and story files; [three planning tracks](https://www.nitorinfotech.com/blog/what-is-the-bmad-method-a-complete-guide-to-ai-driven-software-development-in-2026/) scale process to complexity | Directly overlaps the planning modes and spec-as-context | BMAD is [document-forward](https://charterglobal.com/bmad-method-ai-driven-software-development/) and tooling-bound; no roadmap, forecast or outcome loop |
| [GitHub Spec Kit](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/articles/2025/09/03/github-open-sources-kit-for-spec-driven-ai-development.aspx) (Sep 2025) | Spec, then plan, then small testable tasks for agents; a project "constitution" | Spec as agent context | A tool for one feature. SLICE should recommend it for Mode 2–3 specs, not compete with it |
| [Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.2-chapter-03) | Appetite, shaping, betting, six-week cycles | Appetite and shaping | SLICE runs continuous flow, adds forecasting and evidence levels |
| [Kanban (ProKanban)](https://prokanban.org/forecasting-pocket-guide) | Flow metrics, Monte Carlo forecasting | Flow metrics and F3 | SLICE adds decision and outcome layers |
| [XB Software Functional Slices](https://peerlist.io/slaptick/articles/reinventing-project-management-for-aidriven-development) (Jun 2026) | Value breakdown plus "functional slices"; forecasts slice-days by multiplying a traditional estimate by AI-efficiency and exploration factors | Same unit, near-identical vocabulary | SLICE rejects multiplier estimates; this is a clean, citable contrast |

**Not distinctive (credit, do not claim):** proportional process, vertical slices, appetite, flow metrics, spec-as-context.

**Potentially distinctive (the five things to test):**

1. A Decision Budget that treats throwaway builds as a planning instrument.
2. Verification capacity, not agent count, as the work-in-progress limit.
3. Forecast evidence labels (F0–F3) tied to Direction / Target / Commitment for investors.
4. Outcome hypotheses with kill criteria wired into the roadmap.
5. Measuring decision latency and idea-to-evidence time.

**Public line:** "Use SLICE to decide and forecast. Use any spec tool to execute." Gojko Adzic's [warning that spec-driven development could bring back waterfall rigidity](https://prg.sh/notes/Spec-Driven-Development) is SLICE's opening: SLICE is the discipline that keeps specs small and tied to evidence.

## 4. Principles (revised)

**Core line: "Decide deliberately. Build small. Count only what is proven."** It replaces "Shape only what matters. Build in small slices. Forecast from evidence.", which said nothing about the decision bottleneck.

| Letter | v0.1 | v0.2 | Rule it creates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S | Strategic | Strategic | Every item traces to an outcome or an obligation. |
| L | Lightweight | Lightweight (coordination) | Coordination effort is budgeted and minimised; decision effort is invested on purpose. |
| I | Iterative | Iterative | Only the next slice needs detail. Specs are living documents, updated when implementation teaches something. |
| C | Confidence-led | **Capacity-bound** | Work in progress is limited by human decision and verification capacity, never by the number of agents that can run. |
| E | Evidence-driven | Evidence-driven | Every estimate, date and outcome claim states its evidence level and confidence. Actuals replace assumptions. |

Confidence moves into E, where it belonged. Capacity-bound is the principle the research most clearly supports, and the one most AI-era methods leave out.

## 5. SLICE Roadmapping (revised)

**Every initiative in Now or Next becomes a Bet with a falsifiable outcome and a kill criterion.** The hierarchy (Outcome → Initiative → Feature → Slice → Agent work), the Now/Next/Later horizons and the four item types stay as in v0.1.

### 5.1 The Bet

| Field | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome hypothesis | "If we ship X, metric Y moves from A to B within T." | Self-serve checkout lifts trial-to-paid from 4% to 6% within 8 weeks of release |
| Hypothesis evidence | What the belief rests on today: opinion, interviews, prototype test, live data | 9 of 12 interviewed trial users asked to pay without a call |
| Appetite | The capacity this bet is worth, set before shaping (from Shape Up) | 3 weeks of one builder's verified delivery |
| Leading indicator | A signal readable within 2 weeks of release | Checkout starts per trial |
| Kill criterion | The result that stops or reverses the bet | Under 5% trial-to-paid after 8 weeks, or checkout starts under 10% of trials after 2 |
| Outcome review date | A fixed date, set at release | Release + 8 weeks |

### 5.2 Changes to item types

- **Experiment** now explicitly produces a decision, not software. Throwaway code is expected and is deleted or archived when the decision is made.
- **Delivery** items are only "done" for the roadmap after their outcome review. Accepted delivery counts for forecasting; reviewed outcome counts for the roadmap.
- **Removal** is a legitimate result. When building is cheap, unused features pile up faster; an outcome review that ends in removal is a success of the method, not a failure of the team.

### 5.3 Prioritisation: a fourth rule

v0.1's three rules stay (obligations first, rank by value, urgency and evidence, then set an appetite). Add: **when two bets are close, take the one whose evidence arrives sooner.** In an AI-native team, time to evidence is usually a better tiebreaker than effort, because effort differences have shrunk.

## 6. The Decision Budget (replaces the Planning Budget)

**Cap coordination time; size decision time to the cost of being wrong; and when the open question is about value, build instead of debating.** This is the mechanism that answers the founding thesis directly.

### 6.1 Modes

| Mode | Use when | Output | Illustrative time box |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Direct | Reversible, obvious change | One line of acceptance | 5 minutes |
| 1 Slice brief | Bounded feature, known interfaces | A Slice Card (section 10) | 15–30 minutes |
| **B Build to decide** (new) | The open question is about value or usability: "should we", "which version" | 2–3 throwaway variants shown to users or the decider; one recorded decision | Up to 1 builder-day, then decide |
| 2 Technical shape | Architecture choices, migrations, dependencies | A short decision record plus a spike | 1–2 hours before the spike |
| 3 Assured design | Security, money, privacy, irreversible data, regulation | Reviewed design and test plan | Risk-governed, no ceiling |

The time boxes remain starting policy, not established standards.

### 6.2 Choosing the mode

1. Is the uncertainty about **value** (should we, which one) or about **feasibility and risk** (can we, safely)? Value → Mode B. Feasibility → Mode 2.
2. How costly is reversal? If a mistake reaches customer money, data or trust, Mode 3 overrides everything.
3. Otherwise use the lightest mode that leaves the stop rule answerable.

### 6.3 The stop rule (extended)

v0.1's four questions stay: the outcome or obligation, the smallest useful slice, the most consequential risk, and the evidence of completion. Add a fifth, a plain value-of-information test: **would any realistic answer to the next open question change what we build?** If not, stop planning and start.

### 6.4 Decision rights

Each Bet names one decider. Most decision latency comes from unclear decision rights, not missing information. Every decision is recorded in one line (what, why, who, date) in the repo, so humans and agents read the same record.

### 6.5 Measuring it

v0.1's Planning Overhead Ratio needs everyone to log planning effort, which few teams will do accurately. Replace it with two fields per slice: **coordination minutes** (estimated at close) and **decision latency** (time from a question being raised to its decision being recorded). The ratio can still be derived where data exists.

## 7. Specification as agent context (new)

**Planning output must be readable by agents, not only by people, and it lives in the repository in three layers.** An agent reads the repo and [copies the patterns it finds](https://www.jimmybogard.com/vertical-slice-architecture-webinar-recording-and-whats-next/), so stale or missing context becomes a defect, not a documentation gap.

| Layer | Contents | Changes when | Typical home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product context | Target user, outcomes, glossary, constraints, non-negotiables, design-system rules | Strategy or constraints change | AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, a Spec Kit constitution |
| Decision log | One line per decision: what, why, who, date | Every decision (section 6.4) | docs/decisions |
| Slice spec | The Slice Card plus acceptance evidence, written as tests where possible | Each slice; folded into tests on completion | Beside the branch |

**Rules:**

- Spec size follows the mode: Mode 0 none; Mode 1 the card; Modes 2 and 3 add a decision record and test plan. A longer spec needs a stated reason.
- Acceptance evidence is executable where possible. The test suite is the durable, [machine-checkable form of the spec](https://glukhov.org/ai-devtools/vibe-coding/spec-driven-development-vs-vibe-coding/).
- Living, not frozen: when implementation contradicts the spec, update the spec first, then continue. This is the line between spec-driven work and [waterfall in markdown](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/spec-driven-development-vs-waterfall).
- Every context file has an owner. Context older than the code it describes is flagged in review.
- Any format conforms: Spec Kit, BMAD stories, plain markdown. SLICE specifies what must be present, not the tool.

## 8. SLICE Forecasting (revised)

**Keep v0.1's model; add decision waiting time to the formula, codebase type to the reference class, and minimum evidence to each forecast level.** Size, AI leverage, risk, F0–F3 and P50/P85/P95 stay as defined in v0.1.

### 8.1 Decision wait becomes visible

```latex
T_{\text{slice}} = T_{\text{active}} + T_{\text{review and queue}} + T_{\text{decision wait}} + T_{\text{external wait}}
```

v0.1 hid decision waiting inside "queue" or "external". Separating it lets every forecast show how much of the delay is the bottleneck SLICE claims exists.

### 8.2 Reference classes

A reference class is the combination of size, work type, Decision Budget mode and **codebase context** (greenfield, established, legacy). The Stanford data shows gains several times larger on simple greenfield work than on complex brownfield work, so history from one context never forecasts another.

### 8.3 Minimum evidence per level

| Level | Minimum evidence | Allowed output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F0 | None | "Not estimated" |
| F1 | Expert judgment or analogy | Labelled scenarios (optimistic / expected / conservative), never percentiles |
| F2 | 7 or more accepted slices in the same reference class | Observed range of comparable slices; about 75% chance the next one falls inside it |
| F3 | 11–20 comparable items or 6+ weeks of throughput, from after the last major change, plus at least one back-tested forecast | P50 / P85 / P95 from simulation |

Thresholds follow [Magennis's sampling guidance](https://sep.com/blog/software-forecasting-my-process/) and [ProKanban's history window](https://prokanban.org/forecasting-pocket-guide).

### 8.4 Change resets

A major change in model or tool, team or codebase phase opens a new reference window. Old history becomes a prior, not a forecast basis, until the new window meets its level's minimum. AI tooling changes often enough that this rule will fire several times a year.

### 8.5 Two forecasts per Bet

Each Bet carries a **delivery forecast** (F-level) and a separate **outcome forecast**, which is usually F0 or F1. They are never merged into one confidence figure.

### 8.6 Non-conformant practice

Any estimate of the form "traditional estimate × AI efficiency factor" is non-conformant. Leverage informs the choice of reference class; it never scales a number.

## 9. Verification capacity is the WIP limit (new)

**New agent work starts only when the verification queue has room.** By Little's Law, average cycle time equals work in progress divided by throughput. If review caps throughput, launching more agents only raises WIP and lengthens cycle time, which is what the Faros telemetry shows.

1. **Declare capacity.** State the human hours per week available for review, testing, acceptance and release decisions. Recalibrate monthly.
2. **Pull, not push.** Slices awaiting or under verification may not exceed what that capacity clears in about one week. Above the limit, stop starting and finish.
3. **Reviewable slices.** A slice must be reviewable in one sitting. Oversized diffs are split before review, not after. The 2025 data tied a 154% rise in PR size to a 91% rise in review time.
4. **Verification tier follows risk.**

| Risk | Minimum verification |
| --- | --- |
| Low | Automated tests pass; agent review; human skim of the diff |
| Medium | Automated tests; full human review of the diff against acceptance evidence |
| High (Mode 3) | Independent human review by someone who did not direct the agent; explicit approval for data, money or security changes |

5. **Self-review is never independent.** An agent's or author's own review counts as a check, not as assurance.
6. **No unreviewed merges above Low risk.** The unreviewed-merge rate is reported every cycle (section 11).

## 10. Workflow, cadence and the Slice Card

**The loop now starts and ends with decisions, and verification capacity gates every new build.** v0.1's six steps (Strategy, Shaping, Forecasting, Execution, Acceptance, Learning) become the loop below.

&#91;embedded content: SLICE v0.2 workflow · 6 steps, 1 WIP gate\]

Three of six steps are decision work and two are proof work; only Build got cheap, which is why it is the one step SLICE does not try to optimise.

### 10.1 Cadence

| When | What | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous | Build, verify, release | — |
| Weekly | Flow check: WIP against capacity, review wait, decisions open too long | 20 minutes |
| On each review date | Outcome review: keep, iterate or remove | 15 minutes per Bet |
| Monthly | Bet review: new bets, kills, recalibrate capacity, check reference windows | 60 minutes |
| Quarterly | Strategy and investor update | Half a day |

No ceremony is mandatory; these are defaults for a team of up to about 25.

### 10.2 Slice Card v0.2

The v0.1 card gains a Bet link, the Decision Budget mode, the decider, the reference class, the verification tier and a close-out block. The close-out block is what turns daily work into forecasting history.

```markdown
SLICE CARD · v0.2

Bet: Self-serve checkout (trial-to-paid 4% -> 6% within 8 weeks)
Slice: Test-mode checkout creates the correct entitlement after a confirmed payment
Mode: 3 (money)            Decider: founder
Size: L    Risk: High (consequence)    AI leverage: High
Reference class: L / integration / Mode 3 / established codebase
Acceptance evidence (as tests): successful, failed, delayed-webhook
  and duplicate-webhook payments
Primary uncertainty: webhook ordering and entitlement consistency
Verification: independent human review of payment and data changes
Forecast: 2-3 weeks · F1 scenario · Target
Open decisions: [question · owner · due]

Close-out (at acceptance): coordination minutes · decision latency ·
  cycle time · review wait · rework
```

## 11. Metrics (revised)

**Measure each of the three bottlenecks, not only flow.** v0.1 measured delivery well and decisions not at all. The six metrics marked mandatory are the minimum for a solo founder; the rest are for teams that need them.

| Bottleneck | Metric | Definition | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flow | Accepted throughput | Slices accepted per week, by reference class | Kept, **mandatory** |
| Flow | Cycle time | Start to accepted completion | Kept, **mandatory** |
| Flow | WIP and item age | Items in progress and how long each has been there | Kept |
| Decision | Decision latency | Question raised to decision recorded | New, **mandatory** |
| Decision | Idea-to-evidence time | Bet approved to first leading-indicator reading | New |
| Decision | Coordination minutes | Estimated per slice at close | Replaces planning effort |
| Proof | Review wait | Slice ready to verification started | New, **mandatory** |
| Proof | Unreviewed-merge rate | Share of merges with no human review, by risk tier | New |
| Proof | Rework and escaped defects | As v0.1; add change failure rate from DORA | Kept |
| Outcome | Validated-outcome rate | Share of reviewed Bets whose hypothesis held | New, **mandatory** |
| Outcome | Removal rate | Features removed after outcome review | New |
| Forecast | Calibration | Share of actuals inside the stated P85 (F3) or range (F2) | Kept, **mandatory** |

The v0.1 rule stands: none of these becomes an individual performance target. Validated-outcome rate is the one most likely to be gamed, by writing timid hypotheses, so hypotheses are reviewed for ambition at the betting step.

## 12. Scaling, commitments and investor communication

**Narrow the scaling claim to teams of 1–25 until pilots show otherwise, and print the evidence level beside every date.** v0.1's "solo founder to hundreds of engineers" is untested and invites the wrong first customers.

### 12.1 Scope of the v0.2 claim

SLICE v0.2 is specified for solo founders and teams up to about 25. The 25+ column from v0.1 moves to an appendix marked "untested". Enterprise programmes bring governance that a v0.2 framework cannot credibly specify yet.

| Area | Solo / 2–5 | 6–25 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision rights | Founder decides; decisions logged | One decider per Bet, named on the card |
| Verification capacity | Declared weekly by the founder | Declared per team; review load shared, never pooled into a separate QA queue |
| Forecasting | F1 scenarios until F2 thresholds are met | F2 per reference class; F3 where history allows |
| Reviews | Outcome reviews on their dates; weekly 20-minute flow check | Weekly flow check; monthly bet review |

### 12.2 Commitment rules (tightened)

- Every external date shows its commitment type and evidence level, for example "Self-serve checkout · Target · Q1 · F1".
- A **Commitment** requires F2 or better, or the decider's written acceptance of F1 risk.
- Delivery dates and outcome forecasts are reported on separate lines, as in v0.1.

### 12.3 Investor update, one page

1. Bets in Now and Next, each with its hypothesis and evidence level.
2. Delivery: what was accepted, against the last update's targets.
3. Outcomes: reviews completed this period, validated-outcome rate, removals.
4. Forecast calibration: how the last period's targets compared with actuals.
5. Changes: what moved and why.

## 13. Conformance rules v0.2

**Ten rules; a team that follows all ten practises SLICE, whatever its tools.** v0.1 had seven; the three additions cover decisions, verification capacity and outcomes.

A team practising SLICE must:

1. Keep an outcome-connected roadmap with Now, Next and Later. Every Now and Next initiative is a Bet with a hypothesis, appetite, kill criterion and review date.
2. Choose a Decision Budget mode for each slice, and record coordination minutes and decision latency.
3. Name one decider per Bet, and log decisions in the repository.
4. Keep product context, decision log and slice specs in the repository, each with an owner and current with the code.
5. Split scheduled work into independently verifiable slices, each reviewable in one sitting; decompose XL before scheduling.
6. Record size, dominant risk, AI leverage, reference class and forecast evidence level for each slice.
7. Limit work in progress by declared verification capacity, with no unreviewed merges above Low risk.
8. Label every date with its commitment type and evidence level. Never present judgment as percentiles; never scale an estimate by an AI factor.
9. Count a slice as delivered only when its acceptance evidence passes, and a Bet as done only after its outcome review.
10. Track the six mandatory metrics and use them to change plans.

**Optional:** tools, AI models, cadence, sprints, role names and prioritisation formula.

## 14. Validation plan and how SLICE could fail

**Pilot it on three genuinely different products for eight weeks, with pass/fail thresholds written before the pilot starts.** v0.1's three stages stay; v0.2 adds the hypotheses and the red team.

### 14.1 Pilot hypotheses (thresholds are proposals)

| # | Hypothesis | Passes if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | The Decision Budget shortens decisions | Median decision latency falls 30%+ from the first two weeks to the last two |
| H2 | Less coordination does not cost quality | Coordination minutes per slice fall while rework and change failure do not rise |
| H3 | Forecasts are calibrated | 70%+ of F2 actuals fall inside the stated range; F3 P85 dates are met 80–90% of the time |
| H4 | The outcome loop actually runs | 80%+ of Bets reach their outcome review on the planned date |
| H5 | The WIP rule protects review | Review wait stays flat while accepted throughput rises |

A solo pilot has no control group, so results are before/after evidence only. External pilots (5–10 teams of 1–25 people, 8–12 weeks) should compare against two to four weeks of each team's previous process.

### 14.2 Red team: how SLICE fails

1. **Measurement burden.** Teams will not hand-log decision latency or coordination minutes. Mitigation: derive both from issue and repository timestamps; never require a timesheet.
2. **Outcome reviews get skipped** once shipping feels good, the classic failure of OKR-style systems. Mitigation: the review date is set at release and a Bet cannot close without it.
3. **The card grows.** Bet, Slice Card and reference class can recreate the overhead SLICE fights. Mitigation: if a Mode 1 card regularly takes over 30 minutes, cut fields.
4. **The bottleneck moves again.** If AI review becomes trustworthy, verification capacity rises and decisions dominate even more. SLICE must measure capacity, never assume it.
5. **No moat in ideas.** Every mechanism is copyable and overlaps BMAD, Shape Up and Kanban. Durable value comes from published pilot evidence, templates that live inside the tools teams already use, and a reputation for calibrated numbers.

## 15. Naming and clearance

**Keep SLICE as the working name for pilots, but expect it to be hard to own; decide the public name after the pilots.** The expansion becomes Strategic, Lightweight, Iterative, Capacity-bound, Evidence-driven Execution.

A web check found no existing methodology called "SLICE Framework", but the word is crowded in this exact space:

- [XB Software's Functional Slices](https://peerlist.io/slaptick/articles/reinventing-project-management-for-aidriven-development), an AI-era planning framework built on slices (June 2026).
- [Vertical Slice Architecture](https://www.jimmybogard.com/vertical-slice-architecture-webinar-recording-and-whats-next/), actively marketed as guardrails for AI development.
- The [SLICE research team at LIRMM](https://www.lirmm.fr/teams-en/SLICE-en), Montpellier, working on AI-enabled software engineering.

A common English word that also describes the core unit is weak as a trademark and poor in search. Before public launch: run EUIPO and USPTO searches in classes 9, 41 and 42, check domains, and test whether "SLICE" plus a distinctive qualifier is enough. The decision does not block pilots.
